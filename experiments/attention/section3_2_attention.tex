\subsection{Stochastic computation of the complete attention core}
\label{sec:sc_attention}

We next evaluated the propagation of stochastic errors through the complete
scaled dot-product attention core,
$Y=\mathrm{softmax}(QK^{\mathrm T}/\sqrt{d_k})V$.
The experiment combined stochastic score computation, the joint-output
HI-SMURF operator from Section~\ref{sec:softmax_approximation}, and stochastic
value aggregation. We used 128 independent synthetic input triples with
$n=16$ tokens and $d_k=d_v=32$, and compared the resulting outputs with
floating-point attention. The HI-SMURF parameters were frozen throughout.

We reduced arithmetic sampling error using two simple changes: representing
signed values by a sign bit and a stochastic magnitude stream, and driving
the two operands of each multiplication with distinct coordinates of a
scrambled Sobol sequence. The finite-state machines retained independent
Bernoulli input streams. We also centred the values before aggregation and
restored their mean afterwards, using the identity
\begin{equation}
AV=A\bigl(V-\mathbf{1}\mu^{\mathrm T}\bigr)
   +\mathbf{1}\mu^{\mathrm T},
\qquad
\mu^{\mathrm T}=\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}^{\mathrm T}V,
\label{eq:attention_value_centering}
\end{equation}
which follows from $A\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}$. These changes introduced no
additional trainable parameters.

At a bitstream length of $L=256$ per stage, the improved core achieved an
output root-mean-square error (RMSE) of
0.1092 $\pm$ 0.0008, compared with 0.3135 $\pm$ 0.0014
for the bipolar independent-stream baseline, a reduction of 65.2\%
(mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation across five sequence seeds;
Fig.~\ref{fig:attention_fidelity}a and Fig.~\ref{fig:attention_efficiency}a).
The relative Frobenius error decreased from 0.869 to 0.303,
and the mean cosine similarity between complete output matrices increased
from 0.717 to 0.955. Replacing one stage at a time while evaluating
the remaining stages exactly gave output RMSEs of 0.0098 for stochastic
score computation, 0.1060 for finite-stream HI-SMURF, and 0.0259 for
stochastic value aggregation under the improved configuration
(Fig.~\ref{fig:attention_fidelity}b). The HI-SMURF-only control was close
to the full-core error, identifying nonlinear approximation and finite-state
sampling as the main remaining limitations in this setting.

Increasing $L$ from 64 to 1,024 reduced the improved core's output RMSE from
0.1957 to 0.0715 (Fig.~\ref{fig:attention_efficiency}b), towards the
analytic HI-SMURF error of 0.0534 on the same input matrices.
At $L=128$, the improved core already achieved a lower RMSE
(0.1451) than the baseline at $L=1024$ (0.1712).
This comparison concerns sampling length: each evaluation also used 256
FSM burn-in cycles, and hardware latency was not measured.

The improvement persisted across the tested input regimes, although absolute
accuracy depended on score structure (Fig.~\ref{fig:attention_robustness}a).
For sharper scores generated by doubling $Q$, the improved relative error
was 0.515 and the analytic error was 0.496, revealing a
limitation of the frozen softmax operator under distribution shift.
Value centring addressed a separate source of error. Adding a uniform offset
of 8 to $V$ increased RMSE from 0.1095 to 0.1711 without centring,
whereas the centred implementation retained an RMSE of 0.1092
(Fig.~\ref{fig:attention_robustness}b). The simulation generated actual
bitstreams in all three stages, with floating-point TT contractions and
decoding and re-encoding between stages. Input and output projections were
outside the scope of this attention-core experiment.
